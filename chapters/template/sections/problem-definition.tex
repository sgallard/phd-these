\section{Introduction}
\label{section:template-retrieval-dual-challenge}

\subsection{Motivation}

Automating the retrieval of article templates $\templateobj{j} \in \templatecatalog$ (Chapter~\ref{chapter:definitions}) reduces the manual design overhead of publishing workflows such as \melody's, but selecting from extensive, highly redundant industrial catalogs cannot rely on basic rule-based filtering. How this problem is best formalized, however, depends on the stage of the workflow at which retrieval takes place: whether an article is considered in isolation, or jointly with the other articles already sharing its page.

\subsection{Problem statement}

\paragraph{Single-article setting.} Given a single web article $\articleobj{i}$ considered in isolation and the catalog $\templatecatalog$, this setting aims to retrieve a small candidate set of templates that are simultaneously admissible, structurally diverse, and free from popularity bias. This setting and proposed method are formalized in \secref{sec:trs-problem-formulation}.

\paragraph{Page-aware setting.} Given an article $\articleobj{i}$ evaluated instead as part of a page canvas $\page$ shared with other articles $\articleset = \{\articleobj{1}, \dots, \articleobj{n}\}$, editorial harmony between neighboring templates is the goal, formalized as scoring, rather than retrieving, the compatibility of a candidate template with $\articleobj{i}$, conditioned on its neighbors on the page. This difference is justified since its output is intended to guide the evolutionary layout generation of Chapter~\ref{chapter:pagegen} rather than to be reviewed directly by a human. This setting and proposed method are formalized in \secref{sec:gwes-problem-formulation}.

However, evaluating either setting requires going beyond flat metadata or coordinate-based distances, since both assessing diversity and page-aware scoring hinge on the internal zoning topology $\zoning{j}$ of the templates involved. This motivates the topology-aware similarity metric $\gmn(\cdot, \cdot)$ developed in \secref{section:modelling-similarity}, shared by both settings.

\subsection{Contributions}

Addressing the single-article and page-aware settings introduced above, this chapter makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{tdIoU} (\secref{section:modelling-similarity}): an analytical, distance-aware Intersection-over-Union measure that provides an approximation of structural similarity between templates.
    \item \textbf{tGMN} ($\gmn(\cdot,\cdot)$, \secref{section:modelling-similarity}): a learned Graph Matching Network-based similarity metric, trained on triplets derived from tdIoU thresholds, that captures layout topology beyond flat geometric overlap and is shared across both retrieval settings.
    \item \textbf{\trs} (\secref{section:template-retrieval-single-article}): the Template Retrieval System, a two-phase framework for single-article retrieval that clusters the template catalog offline using tGMN and, balances relevance, structural diversity, and novelty when serving a query in online time.
    \item \textbf{\neuralmethod} (\NeuralMethod, \secref{sec:gwes}): a page-aware scoring metric that combines a Transformer-based editorial intent module with tGMN-based similarity smoothing to correct long-tail bias when evaluating a joint article-template assignment across a shared page.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Outline}

This chapter addresses this dual setting across three sections:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \secref{section:modelling-similarity} develops both tdIoU and tGMN used throughout the chapter.
    \item \secref{section:template-retrieval-single-article} addresses single-article retrieval through the \trs\ system.
    \item \secref{sec:gwes} extends the metric to a page-aware setting via \neuralmethod.
\end{enumerate}
