\section{Evolution of the Press Business Model}
\label{section:newspaper-design}

The digital era has transformed the newspaper industry, introducing new reading experiences across websites, mobile devices, tablets, and digital kiosks~\cite{mitchell-modern-news-consumer-2016, wolf-mobile-news-2015, nozal-newspaper-apps-2020}. This transition opens new opportunities for content distribution and personalization, but it also confronts publishers with a central challenge: preserving the economic and cognitive value of the printed newspaper's two-dimensional layout while producing it affordably within an increasingly content-driven, multi-platform production workflow. The following subsections trace the evolution of the press business model that gave rise to this challenge, and examine the resulting shift in newspaper production.

\subsection{Current situation}

Historically, the press industry relied almost entirely on the physical distribution of print editions, with revenue coming from newsstand sales and print advertising~\cite{picard-free-distribution-2001}. The digital era gradually displaced this model, as advertisers moved their investment toward online platforms. This shift caused a structural decline in print revenue: by 2025, print circulation and advertising combined still represented 43.6\% of publishers' total revenue, while non-traditional ``other'' revenues, such as events, B2B services, and content-licensing deals with AI companies, nearly doubled their share of total revenue, from 13.2\% in 2021 to 25.4\% in 2025~\cite{radcliffe-wan-ifra-2025-2026}. Digital growth has not been enough to offset this decline: between 2020 and 2026, publishers' combined print revenue is projected to fall from US\$68.7 billion to US\$50.1 billion, a loss of US\$18.6 billion, while combined digital revenue grows from only US\$17.5 billion to US\$23.8 billion, a gain of US\$6.3 billion; as a result, the industry's total revenue is expected to fall from US\$86.2 billion to US\$73.9 billion over the same period, a net loss of US\$12.3 billion~\cite{radcliffe-wan-ifra-2025-2026}. On the contrary, some publishers have managed to reverse their dependence on advertising through this same digital shift: \emph{The New York Times}, which drew roughly 80\% of its revenue from advertising less than a decade earlier, came to earn more than 60\% of its revenue from subscriptions~\cite{benson-paywalls-2019}.

   \caption{Global newspaper revenue, 2020--2026. Left: combined print, digital, and total industry revenue. Right: the four underlying revenue streams. Source: \protect\cite{radcliffe-wan-ifra-2025-2026}.}
   \label{ref:business-graphs}
\end{figure}

Even so, this situation did not stabilize on publishers' own websites. Social networks then became the dominant channel for news consumption, further eroding direct web traffic as readers favored aggregated, algorithmically curated feeds over visiting individual newspaper websites. More recently, generative artificial intelligence has introduced a further threat to this already fragile digital revenue base. AI-generated answer summaries placed directly within search engine results, such as Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, reduce the incentive for users to click through to the source article~\cite{lefigaro-apig-ai-overviews-2026}. In France, this led the Alliance de la Presse d'Information Générale (APIG), which represents more than 300 publications, to refer the matter to the French competition authority in 2026, arguing that the feature had been deployed without a fair and transparent negotiation over neighboring rights~\cite{lefigaro-apig-ai-overviews-2026}. Publishers expect search-referred traffic to fall by 43\% over the following three years~\cite{lefigaro-apig-ai-overviews-2026}, a decline that threatens both advertising and subscription revenues, since a shrinking online audience weakens both at once. This confirms that the whole press industry, not only its print branch, faces mounting economic pressure under the current technological landscape.

\subsection{Is print production worth it?}

Given this deepening crisis across both print and digital channels, it is legitimate to ask whether producing the print edition remains worthwhile. This thesis argues that it does, for two reasons.

\begin{enumerate}
    
    \item The print edition is frequently positioned as a premium offering, in comparison to the digital: industry reporting describes the printed newspaper as adapting to an audience demand for curated, collectible experiences that stand in direct contrast to the digital infinite scroll, framing it as an alternative to an increasingly online reading experience~\cite{radcliffe-wan-ifra-2025-2026}. This premium positioning is further reinforced by the digital and AI-driven disruption described above: publishers are increasingly looking for differentiated products whose value cannot be replicated by generative AI systems that summarize or repurpose commoditized web content, and the print edition, with its distinctive two-dimensional layout, is precisely such an alternative. This suggests that a broader ``revival of print'' may become a defining trend of the coming years. A quote from a real publisher supports this claim:

    \begin{quote}
    "{\itshape The link between audience engagement and good design has never been stronger, and it's becoming ever more crucial to commercial success in the digital age}",
    from~\cite{Mor16}
       \label{quote:design-engagement}
    \end{quote}

    \item The value of the newspaper layout extends far beyond physical paper. Its enduring relevance rests on proven cognitive and navigational advantages~\cite{ozretic_dosen_key_2018, hollander_e-reader_2011, holsanova_entry_2006}. Unlike linear digital feeds, layout-based media distribute information across multiple spatially distinct blocks, giving readers structural awareness and a global overview of the content. Entry points such as headlines, subheadings, and visual cues guide attention, foster serendipity, and shape content consumption in ways unique to these documents~\citemine{gallardo-imx}. Simply linearizing articles for responsive digital screens discards this two-dimensional spatial organization. The challenge for the industry, therefore, is not to abandon the layout, but to translate its two-dimensional richness into physical and digital formats alike, without degrading the reading experience. Quotes from real readers support this claims:
    
    \begin{quote}
    "{\itshape I couldn't get the big picture of anything}" \\
    "{\itshape I really like being surprised by [...] finding an article that I wouldn't normally gravitate to, but because of the picture or something, you read it, and you kind of feel enriched because it's something that you wouldn't ordinarily read}",
    readers reacting to an e-reader edition stripped of the print edition's editorial hierarchy~\cite{hollander_e-reader_2011}
    \label{quote:global-awareness}
    \end{quote}

   
 
\end{enumerate}



\begin{callout}[Print as a two-dimensional layout, independent of its physical medium.]
More generally, what matters is not the physical paper itself but the two-dimensional layout in which news content is spatially arranged. This has an important corollary: since the layout paradigm is not tied to a physical medium, it can equally be instantiated digitally. Dynamic, multimodal editions that keep the full richness of digital features, such as embedded video, interactivity, and personalization, while preserving the navigational benefits of the print layout, are therefore a compelling use case. \textbf{Accordingly, throughout this thesis, the term \emph{print} is used to denote either a physical print layout or its digital two-dimensional equivalent, unless stated otherwise.}
\end{callout}



The preceding discussion establishes that producing the print edition remains worthwhile. This holds, however, only if its production costs can be substantially reduced. This economic pressure drives the industry toward the adoption of automated Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) Content Management Systems (CMS).




\section{Print production workflow}

This economic pressure did not only reshape business models: it also transformed the daily operational paradigm of newsroom production. This section examines that operational transformation: it first characterizes the industry-wide shift from layout-driven to content-driven editorial workflows, then introduces \melody, the industrial Content Management System studied throughout this thesis, and finally details the semi-manual web-to-print workflow that \melody currently supports.

\subsection{From layout-driven to content-driven workflows}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\widthForOneImage]{img/layout-driven-vs-content-driven.pdf}
    \caption{Comparison of the layout-driven and content-driven paradigms in newspaper design (Image generated with Google Gemini). (a) layout-driven approach, where articles are written directly into the print layout. (b) content-driven approach, where articles are created independently of their final print presentation.}
    \label{fig:layout-driven-vs-content-driven}
\end{figure}


In the pre-digital era, print journalism was historically tied to the physical page format. Under this traditional ``layout-driven'' approach~\cite{digitizing-news}, journalists wrote articles directly into predefined layouts, bound by the physical space of the printed sheet (\figref{fig:layout-driven-vs-content-driven}a). This spatial constraint limited the length of each article and shaped the writing process around fixed geometric containers, but it also meant that layout composition happened at the same time as content creation.

Digital media shifted newsrooms toward a ``content-driven'' workflow instead, as shown in \figref{fig:layout-driven-vs-content-driven}b. In modern multi-platform newsrooms, editorial content (text, headlines, and multimedia assets) is created independently of its final geometric representation. Journalists now write under a ``web-first'' paradigm, producing stories for digital platforms with no initial layout boundaries. This shift increased publication speed and article volume, but it also created a major challenge for layout-based formats~\cite{newspaper_design_book, newman2024}: unstructured web content must now be fitted, after the fact, into structured two-dimensional layouts for print and digital layout-based editions.

Fitting web articles into structured print canvases is a significant operational burden for modern newsrooms. Because content no longer carries built-in geometric boundaries, editorial teams depend on Content Management Systems (CMS) to format, organize, and assemble it into publication-ready pages~\cite{newspaper_design_book, wagner_trends_2017}. This transition from layout-driven to content-driven workflows defines the environment in which modern CMS platforms operate, and sets the stage for evaluating the industrial solution studied in this thesis.


\subsection{Presentation of Demain Un Autre Jour and \melody}

The CMS studied throughout this thesis to address this transition is \melody, developed by Demain Un Autre Jour (DAJ), a French software publisher specializing in digital technologies for the creation, structuring, transformation, and multi-channel distribution of editorial content~\cite{melody}. Led by CEO Bruno Génuit, an industry expert with over 25 years of experience in press digitalization and editorial workflow management (including prior leadership roles at Ouest-France and IFRA)~\cite{melody}, the company provides software solutions to press organizations, publishing houses, and media groups. The company's flagship product, \melody, was conceived as an intuitive, human-centered Content Management System (CMS) specifically engineered for journalists, freelancers, and newsroom operators. DAJ's primary mission is to streamline the editorial value chain, enabling media organizations to store, reference, and manage their complete information assets, including text streams, photographs, audio, and video, without volume limitations across physical and digital channels~\cite{melody}.

Operating as a web-native Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform, \melody provides a collaborative environment accessible through standard web browsers. Currently deployed across more than 150 newsrooms of varying scales, including major french digital deployments such as \emph{Actu.fr}, the software manages the end-to-end editorial chain from content creation to final publication. The platform's industrial reliability is reflected in its high satisfaction rate of over 95\%~\cite{melody}. 

From an architectural perspective, \melody relies on automated transformation engines that convert native layout formats (such as Adobe InDesign) and legacy digitized archives into fully structured, semantically tagged XML documents. This structured XML foundation enables the logical decoupling of raw editorial content from its geometric layout representation, facilitating multi-channel (web and print) editing across web and mobile platforms. Thanks to this architecture, \textbf{\melody has the ability to bridge digital and physical workflows, enabling editors to generate both web-responsive articles and print-ready newspaper pages} through a user-friendly interface, drastically reducing pre-press production delays. 

However, while \melody provides a robust web-native environment for web and print content management, bridging the gap from  web articles to print pages has historically relied on labor-intensive, semi-manual processes (\figref{fig:melody_manual_editing}). Although the system offers web-based editing tools that allow operators to format web articles into its print format (\figref{fig:melody_manual_a}), and drag-and-drop articles into the page canvas to produce print layouts directly within a browser interface (\figref{fig:melody_manual_b}), \textbf{the workflow lacks automated decision-support mechanisms}. Consequently, operators must manually  produce each page, an arduous task that currently requires hours of manual editing per publication. According to Bruno Génuit, adopting \melody's web-native tooling already reduced average page production time by half compared to earlier, fully manual workflows; the remaining hours of manual editing per publication, however, still represent a substantial operational cost.



\subsection{Specifications of \melody web-to-print workflow}
\subsubsection{Basic elements}
\label{sec:basic-elements}
To formally characterize this manual workflow, it is necessary to first identify the fundamental structural elements manipulated within the \melody environment. To bridge unstructured digital news articles with structured physical  pages, the system relies on four core elements: the \emph{Page}, the \emph{Web Article}, the \emph{Template}, and the resulting \emph{Print Article} (See \figref{fig:page-article-template}). The following provides operational definitions of these elements, which correspond to their formal treatment in Chapter~\ref{chapter:definitions}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\widthForOneImage]{img/page-article-template.pdf}
    \caption{Problem input entities: (a) Page canvas, (b) web article, and (c) template $\templateobj{j}$.}
    \label{fig:page-article-template}    
\end{figure}


\paragraph{Page.} Represents the global geometric container or physical canvas upon which articles are arranged. Industrially, a page is defined by its sheet boundaries, specifically its width  and height, along with outer margins that delineate the effective printable area, as shown in \figref{fig:page-article-template}(a). Within the \melody environment, the page canvas acts as a completely free-form spatial container. While visual grid indicators are available to assist operators during manual layout, the canvas area itself remains unconstrained, serving as the physical two-dimensional space where multiple articles, advertisements, and visual assets must be allocate (\figref{fig:melody_manual_a}).

\paragraph{Web article.} Represents a web article authored by journalists. Operating under a ``content-based'' paradigm, web articles exist purely as digital content without layout topology. Within the web-to-print workflow, each web article is defined by key metadata attributes extracted directly from \melody database which provides its meta-characteristics to produce a print version afterwards (\figref{fig:page-article-template}b): body text character count, total number of associated images, Boolean flags indicating headline or subtitle presence, and an editorial hierarchy flag designating whether the item serves as the main lead story or a secondary piece.

\paragraph{Template.} A \emph{Template} acts as the architectural skeleton or pre-designed layout topology that converts a web article into a multi-column print article. Selected from an extensive catalog $\templatecatalog$ within \melody, a template dictates the internal spatial organization, topology, and geometric zoning of headlines, body text columns, photo placeholders, captions, etc. (\figref{fig:page-article-template}c). 

\paragraph{Print article}
A \emph{print article} represents the concrete instantiation that results from assigning a specific template to a web article to be displayed in the print.

\melody implements the ``web-to-print'' workflow shown in \figref{fig:web-print-workflow}.

The workflow unfolds across two main sequential steps, starting from the input which are web articles:

\begin{enumerate}    
    \item \textbf{Template assignment (Step 1):} To convert a  web article into a two-dimensional layout component, an operator must select first an appropriate design template  from an extensive catalog  within \melody. This selections binds the text and multimedia assets into the template's pre-defined zoning topology, instantiating a print article  with explicit topological characteristics.
    
    \item \textbf{Print layout building (Step 2):} Once individual articles are formatted into print components, the operator moves to the page building phase. In this step, the print articles must be manually allocated and positioned onto the  page canvas. The operator manually adjusts spatial arrangements to ensure that all elements fit within the printable bounds without overlapping, while meeting the visual quality expected from the publication.
\end{enumerate}

Despite the web-native infrastructure provided by \melody, executing this workflow remains a highly labor-intensive process. Step~1 requires human operators to manually search through large template catalogs containing hundreds of highly similar options to find a compatible fit for each article. Subsequently, Step~2 obliges operators to perform complex manual spatial reasoning such as dragging, dropping, and adjusting layout blocks across free-form canvases to achieve a high quality layout without exceeding page constraints. As a result, assembling a single daily edition currently requires hours of dedicated manual editing per publication, creating a bottleneck that increases production costs.


\section{Motivation and Scientific Challenges}
\label{sec:motivation}

\subsection{Business Motivation}

\textbf{This thesis argues that the print production workflow can be automatized  by introducing intelligent, computational frameworks}. Such a shift would accelerate page composition from hours of labor-intensive manual editing to a few minutes of automated execution, all while strictly preserving the visual quality of the publication. This tolerance of a few minutes per page is specified by the industrial partner as the operational requirement for automated composition, since page building is not a real-time task but must fit within the daily publication cycle. Beyond this immediate cost objective, eliminating the remaining production time also opens a longer-term industrial perspective: on-demand, near real-time newspaper production, which neither \melody or any other CMS currently have.

From an industrial and operational perspective, automating layout generation fundamentally transforms the print production. By delegating repetitive geometric fitting, template selection, and spatial packing to computational engines, editors and layout designers are freed from mechanical constraints. Human expertise can thus be reallocated toward high-level creative curation, investigative journalism, and visual storytelling. Economically, this dramatic reduction in production delays enables media organizations to cut operational overhead, increase publication agility, and support multi-platform distribution without incurring unsustainable labor costs. Crucially, this efficiency is achieved without compromising layout quality, thereby protecting brand identity and reader engagement of the print.

\subsection{Societal Motivation}

\textbf{Beyond its economic and industrial impact, this thesis explores a secondary application that addresses a societal challenge: digital reading accessibility for individuals under constrained visual access (related to the device or the individual itself)}. This thesis aims to demonstrate that the same foundational techniques developed for industrial web-to-print automation can be repurposed to synthesize accessible, ``large-print'' digital layouts. By automatically enlarging typographical entry points (e.g., headlines and section headers) while preserving the underlying 2D spatial arrangement, the proposed framework delivers direct-access reading experiences that restore visual scannability, significantly improve reading performance, and reduce perceived cognitive workload.

\subsection{Scientific Challenges}
\label{sec:challenges}

Automating and optimizing the newspaper print production workflow involves bridging computational intelligence, geometric optimization, and human-centered design. Transitioning from traditional, manually orchestrated print layouts to an automated framework introduces several fundamental challenges that span several domains:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Characterization of multiple layout elements:} Newspaper layouts consist of a complex interplay of structured templates and dynamic multimedia assets, including headlines, body text, photographs, and captions. Characterizing these heterogeneous elements and their structural constraints requires a precise understanding of each element characteristics.

    \item \textbf{Combinatorial complexity of spatial packing:} The core layout building task is inherently NP-hard. It requires simultaneously selecting an appropriate structural template for each article from a vast catalog, and determining its spatial coordinates on a two-dimensional page canvas. The coupling of discrete template selection with 2D packing results in an exponential search space growth, making exact global optimization computationally intractable.

    \item \textbf{Balancing multi-objective trade-offs:} Real-world editorial quality cannot be captured by a single scalar objective. Page quality is instead governed by several criteria that answer to different, and partly opposing, interests, so that improving one of them routinely degrades another. Automating layout building therefore requires managing this multi-objective trade-off.

    \item \textbf{Modeling tacit human preferences and editorial style:} Beyond geometric feasibility, automated systems must adhere to subjective aesthetic principles and historical newsroom practices. Formulating objective functions that capture subtle visual balance and implicit human design choices remains a computational challenge.

    \item \textbf{Validating accessibility and user experience benefits:} A key application of automated re-layouting lies in generating accessible large-print editions for readers operating under constrained visual access scenarios (e.g., small mobile displays or low-vision conditions). Verifying whether dynamically generated large-print editions preserve behavioral integrity, reduce navigational costs, and offer superior reading efficiency compared to conventional gesture-based magnification tools (such as pan-and-zoom) requires rigorous empirical validation with diverse user groups.

    \item \textbf{Industrial Deployment and Real-World Scalability:} Finally, transitioning theoretical optimization algorithms into production-ready software systems presents significant engineering hurdles. The developed algorithms must seamlessly integrate into \melody, operate with execution times suitable for daily publication deadlines, and maintain operational stability across diverse publication formats.
\end{itemize}

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

\paragraph{Main objective.} \textbf{Automate the \melody web-to-print workflow, generating a complete print page from a set of web articles without manual template assignment nor manual placement, in a few minutes per page against the hours of manual editing currently required, and with objective values within the ranges observed in operator-produced pages of the same publisher.}

\paragraph{Specific objectives.}

\begin{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Demonstrate} that, in a single-article context, topology-aware template retrieval (Step~1) increases the structural diversity and the long-tail coverage of the retrieved candidate set with respect to usage-driven and topologically-blind baselines, at a bounded loss of conformity to the historical template selections of the operator.

    \item \textbf{Demonstrate} that the proposed multi-objective method to automate the \melody web-to-print workflow, guided by a template selection scoring that extends the introduced topology-aware template retrieval from single-article to page-aware context, attains better trade-offs among the quality measures than constructive and ablated baselines.

    \item \textbf{Validate} that large-print layouts reduce reading time and perceived workload with respect to gesture-based magnification (e.g. pinch and zoom gestures), for readers \NV\ and \LV.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Thesis outline}
\label{sec:thesis-outline}

The remainder of this thesis is organized in two parts. The first part completes the introduction with Chapter~\ref{chapter:sota}, which situates this thesis with respect to the related literature, and Chapter~\ref{chapter:definitions}, which formalizes the page, web article, template, and print article elements introduced in \secref{sec:basic-elements} in precise mathematical terms; this formalization underlies every contribution that follows and is not tied to a single specific chapter. The second part develops the three contributions of the thesis, addressing the main objective and the specific objectives stated in Section~\ref{sec:objectives}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{chapter:template}} addresses the first specific objective. It introduces a topology-aware similarity measure between templates and a retrieval system that, given a single web article, returns from the catalog $\templatecatalog$ a candidate set balancing conformity to historical usage, structural diversity, and long-tail coverage. The chapter closes by extending this retrieval to a page-aware context through \neuralmethod, a neural metric trained on \melody historical assignments that scores template assignments jointly over all articles placed on a page. Although \neuralmethod\ belongs to the template retrieval formalism developed here, its purpose is to serve as one of the quality measures optimized in the following chapter.

    \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{chapter:pagegen}} addresses the main objective, together with the second specific objective. It formulates print layout building as a multi-objective combinatorial optimization problem jointly over template selection and spatial placement, and evaluates the proposed evolutionary method against constructive and ablated baselines in terms of solution quality, feasibility, and execution time.

    \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{chapter:magnification}} addresses the third specific objective. It repurposes the re-layouting principles developed for the industrial workflow to generate large-print editions, and reports a controlled user study comparing reading performance and perceived workload between direct-access large-print layouts and gesture-based magnification, for readers \NV\ and \LV.
\end{itemize}

The thesis closes with a concluding chapter that synthesizes the extent to which the main objective, stated in Section~\ref{sec:objectives}, has been attained, and discusses the industrial and scientific perspectives opened by this work.
